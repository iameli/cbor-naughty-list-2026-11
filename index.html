1 · The number proposal

The proposal makes int a lexicon-level concept and says a CBOR float must never hold a whole number. To test that, every float in the corpus was classified by width, finiteness and whether f == trunc(f).

Why the big ones are unavoidable

A float64 has 52 bits of mantissa, so every value at or above 253 is already a whole number. Under a strict “floats must not be integers” rule, those values become illegal by construction — and past 263 they cannot be represented as an integer at all.

6 · Method and caveats

  • Source. Every sealed Jetstream v2 archive segment, 6.35T of record bytes, replayed through the archive API. Segment payloads are the raw record blocks from the repo CAR — not JSON re-encodings — so float widths, key order and head encodings are exactly what clients wrote.
  • Checker. A hand-written DRISL walker (internal/drisl) that reports every violation in a record rather than stopping at the first, cross-checked against atmos's strict DAG-CBOR decoder on 5.8M records (zero disagreements) and fuzzed with 2.5M random inputs.
  • Counts are exact. Findings are keyed by (record version, path) and de-duplicated on read, so a crash-restart rescan does not inflate them. Scanned-record totals are approximate.
  • Coverage. Deleted records are absent (the archive only holds what exists), and records that were never committed to the firehose do not appear. The audit ran to the sealed tip on 2026-11-12 23:40 UTC.
  • Severity. Only float64_integral, float16, float32, neg_zero and nonfinite conflict with the proposal as written. Everything else here violates the current data model and would still need fixing.
